Conventions

Arrival direction

zenith_angle and azimuth_angle give the arrival direction: the point on the sky the particle comes from, seen from the detector.

  • Zenith: 0 = from overhead, 90 = horizontal, > 90 = up-going (the particle crossed the Earth).
  • Azimuth: measured from geographic north, clockwise seen from above: 0 = from north, 90 = from east, 180 = from south, 270 = from west. This is the astropy AltAz convention and the one of MCEq's location-centred atmospheres. In the local tangent frame x = east, y = north, z = up the unit vector toward the source is (sin z sin a, sin z cos a, cos z).

For positive particles the east–west effect makes cutoffs from the east (azimuth 90) higher than from the west (270).

Converting from other conventions

Source Its azimuth b gtracr azimuth
gtracr < 3.0 0 = from south, 90 = from east (Honda) (180 - b) % 360
Honda / HKKM flux tables 0 = from south, 90 = from east (180 - b) % 360
CORSIKA PHI momentum azimuth, counter-clockwise from magnetic north (x = N, y = W) (180 - b) % 360, then add the magnetic declination for geographic north

East (90) and west (270) are the same in all of these; north and south swap.

Units and constants

  • Rigidity R in GV, R = p c / (|Z| e); momentum p in GeV/c; energy is the total energy in GeV.
  • SI conversions use the exact SI values: e = 1.602176634e-19 C, c = 299792458 m/s, 1 GeV/c = 1e9 e / c kg m/s, 1 GeV/c^2 = 1e9 e / c^2 kg.
  • Earth radius 6371.2 km (the IGRF reference radius); altitudes are above this sphere.
